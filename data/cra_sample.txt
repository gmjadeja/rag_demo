SECTION 1: RRSP CONTRIBUTION LIMITS
================================================================================

Registered Retirement Savings Plan (RRSP) — 2024 Contribution Limits

For the 2024 tax year, the RRSP deduction limit is 18% of your earned income
from the previous year (2023), up to a maximum of $31,560. This is an increase
from the 2023 limit of $30,780.

Your personal RRSP deduction limit is shown on your Notice of Assessment or
Notice of Reassessment from the Canada Revenue Agency. The limit takes into
account any pension adjustments (PA) from your employer's registered pension
plan, past service pension adjustments (PSPA), and pension adjustment reversals
(PAR).

Unused RRSP contribution room from previous years can be carried forward
indefinitely. This means that if you did not contribute the maximum amount in
prior years, you can make a larger contribution in the current year.

The deadline to contribute to your RRSP and deduct it on your 2024 tax return
is March 3, 2025 (60 days after December 31, 2024).

You can contribute to your own RRSP or to your spouse's or common-law partner's
RRSP. Contributions to a spousal RRSP can still be deducted on your own return,
subject to your own RRSP deduction limit.

IMPORTANT: There is no US equivalent under Canadian tax law. Contributions to
a US 401(k) or Roth IRA do not count toward or reduce your Canadian RRSP limit,
and those US plan types have no legal standing under the Income Tax Act (Canada).

SECTION 2: TFSA — TAX-FREE SAVINGS ACCOUNT
================================================================================

Tax-Free Savings Account (TFSA) — 2024 Annual Contribution Limit

The annual TFSA dollar limit for 2024 is $7,000. This is the same amount as
in 2023. The TFSA annual contribution limit is indexed to inflation and
rounded to the nearest $500.

TFSA Cumulative Contribution Room (if eligible since 2009):
- 2009–2012: $5,000 per year = $20,000
- 2013–2014: $5,500 per year = $11,000
- 2015: $10,000
- 2016–2018: $5,500 per year = $16,500
- 2019–2022: $6,000 per year = $24,000
- 2023: $6,500
- 2024: $7,000

If you have been eligible since 2009 and have never contributed, your total
cumulative TFSA room as of January 1, 2024 is $95,000.

Withdrawals from a TFSA are not considered income and do not affect your
eligibility for federal income-tested benefits and credits. Withdrawals are
added back to your contribution room on January 1 of the following year.

Over-contributions to a TFSA are subject to a 1% per month tax on the excess
amount for each month the excess remains in the account. The CRA will assess
this tax automatically based on the information provided by your financial
institution.

SECTION 3: EMPLOYMENT INCOME AND T4 SLIPS
================================================================================

Understanding Your T4 Slip

A T4 slip (Statement of Remuneration Paid) is issued by your employer for
each tax year in which you received employment income. Employers must send
employees their T4 slips by the last day of February following the tax year.

Key boxes on the T4 slip:

Box 14 — Employment Income:
This is the total employment income before any deductions. It includes
salaries, wages, tips, gratuities, bonuses, vacation pay, and taxable
allowances or benefits.

Box 16 — Employee's CPP Contributions:
The amount you contributed to the Canada Pension Plan (CPP) in the tax year.
For 2024, the CPP contribution rate for employees is 5.95% on earnings between
the basic exemption ($3,500) and the maximum pensionable earnings ($68,500).
The maximum annual CPP contribution for an employee in 2024 is $3,867.50.

Box 18 — Employee's EI Premiums:
Employment Insurance (EI) premiums deducted from your pay. For 2024, the EI
premium rate for employees is 1.66% on insurable earnings up to the maximum
insurable earnings of $63,200. The maximum annual EI premium for an employee
in 2024 is $1,049.12.

Box 22 — Income Tax Deducted:
The total federal and provincial/territorial income tax withheld from your
employment income. This is a prepayment of your income tax liability.

Box 52 — Pension Adjustment:
If your employer has a Registered Pension Plan (RPP) or Deferred Profit Sharing
Plan (DPSP), a pension adjustment (PA) is calculated and reported in Box 52.
This amount reduces your RRSP deduction limit for the following year.

SECTION 4: CAPITAL GAINS AND LOSSES
================================================================================

Capital Gains — 2024 Tax Year

In Canada, only a portion of a capital gain is included in your income and
subject to tax. This portion is called the "taxable capital gain" and is
calculated by multiplying the total capital gain by the inclusion rate.

For capital gains realized on or after June 25, 2024:
- The capital gains inclusion rate is 2/3 (66.67%) for corporations and trusts.
- For individuals, the first $250,000 of annual capital gains continues to have
  an inclusion rate of 1/2 (50%). Capital gains above $250,000 in the year are
  subject to the 2/3 inclusion rate.

This change was announced in the 2024 Federal Budget and represents a
significant change to the Capital Gains Tax regime in Canada.

The Adjusted Cost Base (ACB) of a capital property is the original purchase
price plus any costs of acquisition (such as commissions), plus any capital
improvements made to the property. The capital gain is the difference between
the proceeds of disposition (selling price minus selling costs) and the ACB.

Principal Residence Exemption:
If you sell your home and it qualifies as your principal residence for every
year you owned it, the entire capital gain may be exempt from tax. You must
designate the property as your principal residence on Schedule 3 of your
T1 return and file Form T2091(IND).

SECTION 5: GST/HST CREDITS
================================================================================

GST/HST Credit

The Goods and Services Tax / Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST) credit is a
tax-free quarterly payment that helps individuals and families with low and
modest incomes offset the GST or HST that they pay.

To be eligible for the GST/HST credit for the 2024 tax year (July 2024 to
June 2025 payment period), you must:
- Be a Canadian resident for income tax purposes
- Be 19 years of age or older (or have a spouse or common-law partner, or be
  a parent living with your child)
- Have filed a 2023 income tax return

The CRA automatically determines eligibility when you file your income tax
return. You do not need to apply separately.

For the July 2024 to June 2025 period, the maximum GST/HST credit amounts are:
- $519 for single individuals
- $680 for married or common-law partners
- $179 for each child under 19 years of age

These amounts are reduced once net family income exceeds $42,335.

SECTION 6: CANADA CHILD BENEFIT (CCB)
================================================================================

Canada Child Benefit (CCB)

The Canada Child Benefit is a tax-free monthly payment made to eligible families
to help with the cost of raising children under 18 years of age.

For the 2024–2025 benefit year (July 2024 to June 2025), the maximum annual
CCB payments are:
- $7,787 per child under age 6
- $6,570 per child aged 6 through 17

These maximum amounts apply to families with net income below $36,502. The
benefit is reduced based on family net income above this threshold using a
benefit reduction formula specified in section 122.61 of the Income Tax Act.

The CCB may include the Child Disability Benefit (CDB) for families caring for
a child with a severe and prolonged impairment in physical or mental functions.
The maximum CDB for 2024–2025 is $3,322 per child.

To receive the CCB, both you and your spouse or common-law partner (if
applicable) must file income tax returns every year, even if you have no income.

SECTION 7: FOREIGN INCOME AND REPORTING REQUIREMENTS
================================================================================

Reporting Foreign Income and Assets

Canadian residents must report their worldwide income on their T1 General
Income Tax Return, including income from foreign sources such as:
- Foreign employment income
- Foreign pension income (including US Social Security benefits)
- Foreign investment income (dividends, interest)
- Foreign rental income

Foreign Tax Credit:
If you paid income tax to a foreign country on income you also report in Canada,
you may be able to claim a foreign tax credit to avoid double taxation. Use
Form T2209 (Federal Foreign Tax Credits) to calculate this credit.

Note: US Social Security payments received by Canadian residents are treated
as pension income in Canada and must be reported. Under the Canada-US Tax
Treaty (Article XVIII), only 85% of US Social Security benefits are included
in income for Canadian tax purposes.

Foreign Asset Reporting (T1135):
If you owned foreign property with a total cost of more than $100,000 CAD at
any time during the tax year, you must file Form T1135 (Foreign Income
Verification Statement). This applies to:
- Bank accounts held outside Canada
- Shares of foreign corporations (not held in registered accounts)
- Foreign real estate (not for personal use)
- Interests in foreign partnerships or trusts
- Other foreign property

The deadline for filing T1135 is the same as your income tax return.
Penalties for late filing or non-compliance can be significant.

SECTION 8: MEDICAL EXPENSE TAX CREDIT
================================================================================

Medical Expense Tax Credit (METC)

You can claim eligible medical expenses paid for yourself, your spouse or
common-law partner, and your dependent children (born in 2007 or later).

Eligible medical expenses must exceed the lesser of:
- 3% of your net income (line 23600 of your T1 return), OR
- $2,635 (for 2024)

The credit is calculated at 15% of the eligible medical expenses above this
threshold.

You can claim any 12-month period ending in the current tax year. Strategically
choosing your 12-month claim period can maximize the credit, especially if
expenses were concentrated in one part of the year.

Common eligible medical expenses include:
- Prescription drugs and medications (prescribed by a licensed medical
  practitioner)
- Dental services (not covered by insurance)
- Vision care (eyeglasses, contact lenses)
- Hospital services
- Medical travel expenses (if you travelled more than 40 km to get medical
  services not available locally)
- Attendant care costs for persons with disabilities

Cosmetic procedures that are not medically necessary are generally NOT eligible
as medical expenses.

SECTION 9: DISABILITY TAX CREDIT (DTC)
================================================================================

Disability Tax Credit (DTC)

The Disability Tax Credit is a non-refundable tax credit that reduces the
amount of income tax a person with a severe and prolonged physical or mental
impairment may have to pay.

For 2024, the federal DTC amount is $9,428 (base amount). The credit is
calculated at 15%, resulting in a maximum federal tax reduction of $1,414.20.
An additional supplement of $5,500 is available for persons under 18, subject
to a reduction for child care and attendant care expenses claimed.

To be eligible, a medical practitioner must certify that you have a severe and
prolonged impairment using Form T2201 (Disability Tax Credit Certificate).
The CRA reviews and approves DTC applications on a case-by-case basis.

If you are eligible for the DTC but do not owe enough taxes to use the full
credit, you may be able to transfer the unused portion to a supporting person
(such as a spouse, parent, or sibling) using Schedule 2 of the T1 return.
